    <p>People now meet images, audio, video and text that may be synthetic, edited, or simply pulled out of context. Most public guidance still reduces this to one question, whether the thing is real. That question gets harder to answer as generation improves, and it hides a more common problem. A Reuters Institute analysis of fact-checked COVID-19 misinformation found that about 59% of items were genuine material that had been reconfigured, while about 38% were fabricated. Most misleading media does not need AI at all.</p>
    <p>AI also sits on both sides of the encounter. It may have produced what you are looking at, and it is increasingly the tool people reach for to check it. A reasoning habit has to survive both facts, and it has to survive the next generation of tools.</p>

## The programme

STOP&SCAN was created inside the ITU AI for Good AMAS Young Researcher Associate Programme (YRAP). AMAS is the AI and Multimedia Authenticity Standards Collaboration, a joint effort of the ITU, ISO and IEC that brings together standards bodies, industry and researchers working on how to establish and communicate the authenticity of media. The YRAP pairs early-career researchers with mentors from across that community and asks them to produce work that moves from analysis to something the public can use.

The 2026 cohort organised itself into three working groups:

- **Social sciences and education.** My group. It owns the STOP&SCAN framework, its research, and the Amito mascot and brand.
- **Technology.** Builds the Amito toolkit, a chat assistant that turns the framework into something people can talk to.
- **Policy.** Works on the policy side of multimedia authenticity.

The project was showcased at the AI for Good Global Summit 2026 in Geneva as one of several cohort projects sharing what the programme learned in its first year. The ITU covered the cohort in a feature titled "From deepfakes to digital trust". STOP&SCAN is a project of the AMAS YRAP 2026 cohort, in partnership with the ITU, AI for Good, RIT's ESL Global Cybersecurity Institute and the University of Bologna.

## The framework

### What changed since the first version

The original page described a five-step sequence at the level of a slogan. The framework has since been through a pilot survey, a literature synthesis, and a two-round expert panel, and several parts have been rewritten as a result. The current version differs from the first in these ways:

- **Entry question:** There is now an entry question that decides whether the check starts at all, so that nobody is asked to fact-check every post they scroll past.
- **Effort calibration at STOP:** STOP sets the effort for everything that follows, in three levels of light, moderate and deep. Effort is defined by how far each step goes and never by how long it takes, because a clock adds the pressure that STOP is meant to relieve.
- **Fixed sequence:** The sequence never shortens. Whatever the effort level, a person runs Source, Content, Alignment and Now Reflect in order and to the end. Experts and prior work both showed that people judge "enough checking" badly, and bias is strongest at that moment.
- **Concrete alignment rule:** Alignment has a concrete rule. A question is settled only when at least two independent pieces of evidence point the same way.
- **Comparative reflection:** Now Reflect is two questions that compare the result with the reaction noted at STOP and then turn it into an action.
- **Separating evidence from action:** Evidence and action are kept apart. What the evidence says is one decision, and what to do about it depends on whether the content is telling, asking or warning.

### The five steps

#### Entry question

Two questions decide whether to start: **Do I care to engage with this**, meaning act on it, share it, react to it or take it in as true? And **does it change what I think about someone or something?** If both answers are no, scroll on and nothing else is asked.

### The effort ladder

Effort is defined by how far each step goes. Each level adds to the one before:

| Step | Light | Moderate | Deep |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Source** | The account that posted what you see, past any resharer | The account's record | The first poster, who spreads the claim, and what they gain |
| **Content** | Open the post or link and read past the headline. Does the content show what the caption claims? Zoom in on one detail such as a name, a date or a place | Check whether the details hold up with a search, a map, or the weather and news for the date. Note what is left out. Name any pressure trick such as a rush, strong emotion, or someone posing as a person you trust | Look closely at the image, video or sound itself, frame by frame or with a metadata viewer or detector. Treat anything odd as a lead for Alignment, since eyes and single tools miss good fakes |
| **Alignment** | Two independent pieces point the same way | Two independent pieces point the same way | Two independent pieces point the same way, and the strength check rules out the most likely other explanation |
| **Now Reflect** | The same two questions | The same two questions | The same two questions |

### From evidence to action

What the evidence says and what to do about it are separate decisions. The framework keeps them apart:

| Content Type | Supported | Not sure yet | Contradicted |
|---|---|---|---|
| **It tells you something** | Rely on it, and share it if it helps others | Hold. Do not share it and do not dismiss it | Do not share it. Correct it if you are placed to, and report it if it is harmful |
| **It asks you to do something** | Go ahead | Check through a channel the requester does not control | Refuse and report |
| **It warns you** | Act as directed | Take the step you can undo and look for the official channel | Stand down and report |

### Why it is called metacognitive

Stop and Now Reflect are the two points where a person monitors their own judgement. The first records the reaction, and the last compares where they landed with that reaction before they act. Source, Content and Alignment are the object-level checks in between. The framework does not claim that every step is metacognitive. It claims that the whole sequence puts a monitoring and control loop outside the head, where people who do not run such a loop unaided can use it. That is also why evidence is reported as supported, contradicted or not sure yet, and never as a confidence score. Lay confidence is a poor signal of accuracy, so the framework replaces it with a coarse measure anchored in evidence you can point to.

### How it relates to SIFT

STOP&SCAN builds on SIFT and lateral reading and is meant as a contemporary successor for an environment where AI may have made the artifact and may also be the checking tool. It differs in four ways:

1. It always runs the whole sequence with no early exit.
2. It requires two independent pieces of evidence before calling a question settled.
3. It treats tool results and AI answers as clues and never as verdicts.
4. It separates the evidence state from the action that follows.

- **Pilot survey:** In June and July 2026 the team surveyed 46 adults from more than 15 countries about their concerns over fake information online and how well they felt they could detect it. It grounded the project in what people actually worry about.
- **Expert panel:** The framework was evaluated with a modified Delphi method. In Round 1, sixteen practitioners from media literacy, fact-checking, human factors, machine learning and media forensics, based in Europe, North America, Western Asia and Africa, worked through four short fictional cases in which a character applies the framework. They rated each step, wrote critiques, and compared STOP&SCAN with SIFT. We turned the most consistent concerns into eleven proposed revisions, each with a stated reason. In Round 2, ten of the experts rated each revision, and all eleven met the agreement rule of at least 70% rating it 4 or 5 on a five-point scale with a narrow spread. The panel judged the design. It did not test whether the public benefits, and the paper says so.
- **Next steps:** A three-study evidence programme with the public, covering how people perceive the problem, how learnable the framework is, and what effect it has on judgement and sharing.

[stopandscan.org](https://stopandscan.org/) is the public home of the framework. I designed and built it so that the framework could be used and not only read about. It has no sign-up and costs nothing to use:

- **Learn:** A guided lesson in which Amito walks through the five steps on a real-world style case. The current lesson is a celebrity investment video, takes about eight minutes, gives feedback on your scan, and lets you retake it.
- **Practice:** Case studies to apply the steps to.
- **Resources:** One page per step with plain-language guidance.
- **Comics:** Illustrated stories that show the framework at work in the noise of the internet.
- **My Journal:** A place to keep your own reflections.
- **Meet Amito:** The mascot's guide, including the Telegram link to the Amito toolkit.
- **Style guide:** The colours, type, components and Amito poses, open to anyone building with the brand.
- **Slide deck:** The slides for the framework and Amito live at [slides.stopandscan.org](https://slides.stopandscan.org/).

### Tech stack

The stack is deliberately small, in contrast to the DeFake application:

- **Astro** for a fast, mostly static site with interactive islands where needed.
- **Tailwind CSS** with brand colors, spacing, and radii defined as unified tokens.
- **Typography:** Outfit, Nunito Sans, and Gochi Hand for display, body, and Amito's handwritten speech.
- **WebP assets** for lightweight delivery of Amito poses and comics.
- **Cloudflare** for hosting and edge delivery.
- **Vite** for the presentation deck at slides.stopandscan.org.
- Zero server database or mandatory tracking, ensuring high speed, cost-effectiveness, and smooth handoff for the cohort.

### Amito

Amito is the guide. The site describes Amito as the messenger of trust, a companion who changes with you through each step of the framework and signals the cues that keep the journey safe. The point of the character is that a habit sticks when it has a face. A mascot lets the framework say "stop" without sounding like a warning label, and lets it admit "I don't know yet" without that sounding like failure.

Amito is a small robot with a soft cream body, a visor face that shows expression, and a lilac head mark that is an A built from an upward arrow. The body is part of the framework. Amito's glow changes with the step:

- **Stop:** Pink-red torso glow
- **Source:** Cyan right cuff, marked S
- **Content:** Green left cuff, marked C
- **Alignment:** Lilac head logo
- **Now Reflect:** Orange torso glow

The facial expressions were designed as carefully as the colours. Amito never scolds. In the Stop pose Amito looks uneasy, with a raised bead of sweat, because the point of Stop is that the content has already pulled on you and nothing is wrong with you for feeling it. This is what the site means by helping you pause without judgment.

### Key decisions

- **Sequence reasoning steps instead of shipping another detector:** Detection tools age as generative models improve. A reasoning sequence that uses whatever signals exist at the time lasts longer. Alternative considered was a checklist tied to specific platform features or model artifacts.
- **Let effort vary and never let the path shorten:** Experts asked for effort that fits the stakes, so STOP sets a light, moderate or deep level. The order and the end of the sequence never change, because people are poor judges of when they have checked enough. Alternative considered was an early exit once one check looked convincing.
- **Give the framework a mascot and a brand:** A consistent visual identity turns a five-step habit into something people want to engage with and share, and Amito also fronts the toolkit. Alternative considered was a plain explainer page.
- **Keep brand, product and media under one hand:** With a small, dispersed group and a hard summit deadline, one person owning the mascot, brand, website and video kept the experience consistent while the research and the technical and policy teams ran in parallel.

### Impact

STOP&SCAN shipped as a public framework, brand and website, with a cohort introduction video, in time for the AI for Good Global Summit 2026 in Geneva. Since then the framework has been revised through an expert panel, a research paper is in submission, and the work is being taken to standards and education communities. It gives the programme a concrete, testable public-education artifact rather than only a research readout.

### Learnings

- Asking "how is this trying to influence me?" and "how settled is the evidence?" is more teachable and more durable than any real or fake classifier.
- Experts were least convinced that a non-expert can tell whether two pieces of evidence are independent, which is why the rule is now paired with worked examples and kept deliberately simple.
- "Not sure yet" has to be made worth something, so it comes with a safe step and a note on what would settle the rest.
- Current AI video generation tools are not yet reliable enough for polished, controllable short-form video, and hands-on production was still faster and better.
- A mascot and a consistent brand turn an abstract cognitive framework into something people can adopt as a habit.
