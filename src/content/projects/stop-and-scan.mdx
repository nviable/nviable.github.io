## Case narrative

### Problem

People increasingly encounter content — across text, image, audio, and video — that could plausibly be manipulated, but most public guidance still reduces the problem to a single yes/no detector question. That framing breaks down as synthetic media keeps improving, and it doesn't teach a transferable reasoning habit that survives the next generation of tools.

### Constraints

- A small, cross-disciplinary working group (social sciences, psychology, policy) spread across institutions and time zones, coordinating with parallel technical and policy sub-teams under the same AMAS programme
- Had to work for a general public audience, not researchers — no jargon, low reading effort, designed as a repeatable habit rather than a one-time lesson
- Needed to stay durable against future detection-tool and platform changes instead of being tied to any single model or signal
- Hard deadline: framework, brand, website, and video all needed to ship in time for the AI for Good Global Summit 2026 showcase

### Approach

Reframe the question from "is this real?" to "how is this trying to influence me?" — then scaffold that reframing into five ordered steps — Stop, then Source, Content, Align, Now-reflect (SCAN) — a person can run through in seconds. Pair the framework with a mascot-led product (Amito) so the habit reads as approachable rather than clinical, and ship it as a real website with illustrated learning content rather than a slide deck or PDF guide. The public framing keeps it memorable: *pause before you trust, scan before you share, reflect before you act.*

### Key decisions

**Sequence reasoning steps instead of shipping another detector** — Detection tools age quickly as generative models improve; a reasoning sequence that incorporates whatever signals exist at the time stays useful for longer. Alternative considered: a checklist tied to specific platform features or model artifacts.

**Give the framework a mascot and brand, not just a methodology** — Amito and a consistent visual identity turn a five-step cognitive habit into something people actually want to engage with and share, rather than another compliance-style warning label. Amito is more than a face, too: the name stands for the *AI for Good Media Integrity Toolkit* — the cohort's companion chatbot — so the character also fronts real tools people can turn to for help, not just a lesson. Alternative considered: a plain, unbranded explainer page.

**Keep brand, product, and media under one hand for coherence** — With a small, dispersed group and a hard summit deadline, having one person own the mascot, brand, website, and video kept the public-facing experience visually and tonally consistent — while Fatma Aksu led the framework research and the technical and policy sub-teams drove their parts.

### Tech stack

Web front-end tooling, illustration & branding tools, AI video-generation tools (evaluated for the cohort introduction video — found still well short of what's advertised for consistent, controllable output).

### Impact

STOP&SCAN shipped as a public framework, brand, and website ahead of Session 1 (AMAS Young Research Associate Programme) at the AI for Good Global Summit 2026 in Geneva — one of several cohort projects sharing lessons from the programme's first year. It gives the programme a concrete, testable public-education artifact rather than only a research readout: something the group can put in front of real people and keep iterating on.

### Learnings

- Framing content skepticism as "how is this trying to influence me?" is more teachable and more durable than any real/fake classifier
- Current AI video-generation tools aren't yet reliable enough for polished, controllable short-form video — hands-on production is still faster and better
- A mascot and consistent brand turn an abstract cognitive framework into something people will actually adopt as a habit
