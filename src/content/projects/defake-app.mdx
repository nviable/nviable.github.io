## Case narrative

### Problem

Off-the-shelf deepfake detectors are black boxes. Journalists, intelligence analysts, and investigators need explainable cues, provenance context, and interfaces that match how they already work under time pressure — not a single probability bar.

### Constraints

- Stakeholders span newsrooms, government, and academia with different threat models
- Models and attack surfaces evolve faster than traditional release cycles
- Human-in-the-loop judgment must stay central to avoid automation bias
- Funding and compliance requirements across multiple sponsors

### Approach

Mixed-methods research (interviews, diary studies, workshops, usability tests) drives requirements and iteration. High-fidelity Figma prototypes are tested before engineering commits. The stack combines Flask/React services with PyTorch/Keras models, Dockerized components, and design patterns that surface evidence (intra-frame, inter-frame, audio, speaker identity) in a forensic-style UI rather than a single verdict.

### Key decisions

**Prioritize explainable, multi-signal UI over a single authenticity score** — Expert users need to justify conclusions to editors and courts; layered evidence reduces harmful over-trust in one number. Alternatives: minimal UI wrapping a single classifier output; fully automated moderation pipeline.

**Co-design with journalists and analysts from the first prototype** — Workflow fit and trust are as important as raw AUROC; field studies surfaced needs no lab study would catch. Alternatives: ship a demo first and collect feedback post-launch; rely only on academic peer review for validation.

### Tech stack

Python, Flask, React, PyTorch, Keras, Figma, Docker.
