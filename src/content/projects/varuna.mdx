## Case narrative

### Problem

Analysts and reporters collect heterogeneous signals — metadata, visual artifacts, audio, identity cues — across many tools with no shared vocabulary or navigation model. That slows the work and makes collaboration and training harder than they need to be.

### Constraints

- Classified-adjacent workflows mean prototypes and studies need careful review
- The ontology must stay usable, not become an academic-only artifact
- Integration burden is high across models and legacy analyst tools

### Approach

Ground the ontology in interviews and iterative design with intelligence analysts and journalists, then build a goal-oriented UI that maps tasks (e.g. establish provenance, compare versions) to toolchains and evidence objects. Align engineering with DeFake model services and the shared design system wherever possible.

### Key decisions

**Anchor the product on a shared ontology rather than a flat tool menu** — A structured map of forensics concepts supports explainability, training, and future automation without hiding human judgment. Alternatives considered: integrate tools via shallow deep links only; rely on vendor-specific taxonomies per integration.

**Co-lead across HCI, security, and partner agencies from early sketches** — Reduces the mismatch between what analysts say they need and what actually ships. Alternative considered: waterfall requirements from a single stakeholder group.

### Tech stack

Python, Flask, React, Figma, Docker.

### Impact

Varuna formalizes how analysts and journalists reason about media evidence into a shared, reusable ontology — the basis for CHI-track research on the Visual Media Forensics Ontology and for related work with intelligence analysts. The underlying ontology was briefed to DARPA in 2024, connecting the project's academic research directly to defense and national-security applications. As a platform, Varuna moves DeFake's research toward analyst-grade workflows and forensic concepts that can be reused across tools, training, and future automation.

### Learnings

- Ontologies only help if the UI rewards experts for using them — hide the graph when it gets in the way
- Pair ontology work with continuous validation from practitioners, or it drifts from real workflows
