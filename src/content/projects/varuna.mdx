## Case narrative

### Problem

Analysts and reporters collect heterogeneous signals (metadata, visual artifacts, audio, identity cues) across many tools with no shared vocabulary or navigation model — slowing work and making collaboration and training harder.

### Constraints

- Classified-adjacent workflows mean prototypes and studies need careful review
- The ontology must stay usable, not become an academic-only artifact
- Integration burden across models and legacy analyst tools

### Approach

Ground the ontology in interviews and iterative design with intelligence analysts and journalists; build a goal-oriented UI that maps tasks (e.g. establish provenance, compare versions) to toolchains and evidence objects. Align engineering with DeFake model services and design system where possible.

### Key decisions

**Anchor the product on a shared ontology rather than a flat tool menu** — A structured map of forensics concepts supports explainability, training, and future automation without hiding human judgment. Alternatives: integrate tools via shallow deep links only; rely on vendor-specific taxonomies per integration.

**Co-lead across HCI, security, and partner agencies from early sketches** — Reduces mismatch between what analysts say they need and what ships. Alternatives: waterfall requirements from a single stakeholder group.

### Tech stack

Python, Flask, React, Figma, Docker.
