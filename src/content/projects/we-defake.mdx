## Case narrative

### Problem

Investigations span organizations and time zones, but mainstream social products are poor fits for forensic evidence handling, chain-of-custody norms, and expert-only discussion.

### Constraints

- Federation adds moderation, identity, and abuse-surface complexity
- The platform had to interoperate with existing DeFake apps without forcing a single host
- Community size was always likely to stay small — a reason to design for depth over viral growth

### Approach

Deploy and customize Pleroma/Soapbox with workflows that encourage structured posts (claims, sources, tool outputs) and cross-link to DeFake analysis where appropriate. Iterate with a small group of researchers and journalists rather than chasing open registration.

### Key decisions

**Use ActivityPub / Pleroma rather than a bespoke real-time-only stack** — Standards-based federation lets institutions run their own nodes and interoperate, reducing lock-in. Alternatives considered: a closed Slack/Discord-style workspace only; a greenfield custom social graph.

**Position We.DeFake as expert-adjacent, not a consumer social network** — Keeps expectations and moderation scope aligned with forensic use. Alternative considered: open registration for the general public.

### Tech stack

Pleroma, Soapbox, ActivityPub, Elixir, Docker.

### Impact

We.DeFake served as a working probe for a specific question: can open, federated infrastructure host the kind of careful, evidence-centered collaboration that forensic work demands? Standing up and customizing a Pleroma/Soapbox instance for expert use showed where federation helps — institutional autonomy, no single point of control, standards-based interoperability — and where it struggles: moderation load, identity assurance, and onboarding people into forensic norms. The project is now inactive, but those findings inform how the rest of the DeFake ecosystem approaches collaboration and trust.

### Learnings

- Federated communities need clear governance and onboarding for forensic norms from day one
- Technical federation does not automatically create trust — social design matters more than the protocol
