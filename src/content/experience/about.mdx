## Overview

I am a human–computer interaction and digital media forensics researcher focused on building usable, trustworthy deepfake detection tools for journalists, intelligence analysts, and law enforcement. I founded and lead the DeFake Project — an interdisciplinary initiative across four universities — and work at the boundary of qualitative inquiry, interface design, and machine learning deployment.

Across 7+ years in HCI and ML research and 4+ years of UXR and product-facing industry work, I have planned and executed end-to-end mixed-methods studies, shipped interactive prototypes, and translated findings into strategy for faculty PIs, engineering teams, and practitioner partners.

## How I work

I combine field interviews, diary-style exercises, workshops, usability tests, and surveys to understand verification workflows under real constraints (time pressure, organizational norms, and automation bias). I communicate through academic publications, talks, workshops, interviews, and close collaboration with tool builders.
